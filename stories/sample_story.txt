The Last Local

The 11:40 local from Churchgate was almost empty when Meera stepped in, clutching a brown envelope to her chest. Rain hammered the roof of the coach. She chose a seat by the window and tried to steady her breathing.

At Dadar, an old man in a damp kurta sat down across from her. He looked at the envelope, then at her face.

"You are Sharma ji's daughter," he said quietly. "Don't be afraid. My name is Raghunath. I worked with your father for thirty years."

Meera pulled the envelope closer. "How do you know who I am? Who sent you?"

"Nobody sent me, beta. Your father told me that if anything ever happened to him, you would be on this train, carrying that."

Her phone buzzed. It was her brother, Kabir. She answered, and his voice came through loud and frantic. "Meera, where are you? Do not give those papers to anyone! I am coming to Andheri station, just stay on the train!"

"Kabir, there is a man here. He says he knew Papa."

"Listen to me!" Kabir shouted. "Papa trusted nobody at that office. Nobody!"

The line went dead. The train slowed between stations and the lights flickered. Raghunath leaned forward and lowered his voice to a whisper.

"Your brother is right to be careful. But ask him one thing when he arrives. Ask him why he was at the office the night your father died."

Meera stared at him. The rain had stopped. In the sudden silence, she could hear her own heartbeat.

"That is not possible," she said, her voice breaking. "Kabir was in Pune that night. He told me so himself."

Raghunath smiled sadly and stood up as the train pulled into Bandra. "Then you already know which one of us is lying."

He stepped onto the platform and disappeared into the crowd.
